# Hydris: assets to collect for the cinematic website

Based on the current website at http://localhost:4307/ and its source at `E:\Startup\Hydris\website\hydris-hybrid`, reviewed 10 October 2026.

The site already has Vetri Dhagumudi's founder story, the Operational Water Intelligence positioning, three products, an expert knowledge base, a learning-loop narrative, and substantial visual effects. The missing material is primarily real product evidence, original people imagery, a dated company archive, and attributable proof.

Do not wait for every item in this document. The minimum pack below is enough to make the first redesign more specific. Existing photographs and the cine film can support the atmosphere until original material is available.

## Minimum pack to provide first

1. **One product truth sheet:** Lite/Pulse/Field purpose, actual features, availability, what each can demonstrate today, hardware requirements, and the primary CTA/assessment offer. Explain the hardware exception for Field, what “day one” means, and which existing deployment/data-handling claims are approved.
2. **Six useful screens:** about two per product if available. Prioritize one overview and one detail. If Field is not a screen-based product, provide guidance-view captures plus actual equipment/workflow imagery. Label each as production, prototype, or concept.
3. **One complete operating example:** issue → inputs → relevant expertise/evidence → reasoning/uncertainty → suggested check → operator decision → recorded outcome, if available. State whether it is real, anonymized, or illustrative.
4. **Three founder photographs:** one portrait, one environmental work portrait, and one candid working image of Vetri. Provide approved captions; the biography is already present.
5. **One team photograph and two candid images:** actual contributors, with names/roles only where approved for publication.
6. **Four to six dated Hydris milestones:** the real chronology, with at least two original artifacts such as a sketch, early screen, notebook, plant-visit photograph, or prototype image.

Audio, custom 3D, a full documentary, and a large stock-media purchase are not prerequisites.

## How the photographs should feel

Aim for an editorial record of people who know the work: natural side light, calm expressions, real environments, and modest colour treatment. Faces and hands should stay clear. Choose images that tell different parts of the story rather than five variations of the same pose.

For Vetri, photograph a direct portrait, a seated or standing environmental portrait, and a real working moment. Keep the background simple and relevant. Do not digitally replace the founder with a generated face. A portrait does not need a suit, dramatic smoke, or an artificial science-fiction control room to communicate authority.

For the team, capture people discussing a plant problem, looking at a real interface, reviewing a diagram, or testing a workflow. If the team is distributed, show that honestly. Avoid a composite photograph that implies everyone was physically together when they were not.

For operators, prioritize an action that relates to the narrative: reading, interpreting, checking, deciding, recording. Images of random machinery cannot show human judgment by themselves.

## Product capture instructions

- Use one coherent sanitized demo plant across Lite, Pulse, Field, and the recorded scenario when possible.
- Capture the native interface at its normal scale. Keep text readable; export full images before making crops. Avoid photographing a monitor unless the physical setting matters.
- Remove customer names, credentials, private files, and sensitive operating details before supplying public-use assets. Use approved sample data and clearly identify synthetic/illustrative captures.
- Record the sequence the visitor should understand, not an aimless tour of tabs. Start with the operating question, open the evidence, show the reasoning and confidence/limits, then show the operator's next step and observation record.
- For each file, add product name, software version/date if relevant, production/prototype/concept status, what the visitor is seeing, and the one point the image supports.
- Field screenshots need a view of what the user actually sees. A photograph of generic smart glasses alone is insufficient to demonstrate guidance or observation capture.
- If a product is not ready for public demonstration, provide an explicitly labelled prototype or omit the missing screen. Do not ask Claude to manufacture a “real” dashboard.

Keep clips stable. Leave a few seconds before and after the action. Supply clean footage without baked titles, flashy transitions, or compulsory music; the website can control crops and sequence. Use still posters on constrained devices instead of forcing every clip to autoplay.

## Music direction

The useful direction is quiet, spacious, and instrumental: warm ambient pads, restrained piano or tonal texture, gentle pulse if any, and soft water/plant ambience. Roughly 65–85 BPM can work, but rhythm is optional. The motion sequence should work independently of the soundtrack.

Avoid epic trailer drums, synthetic alarm sounds, prominent vocals, corporate ukulele, or constant click effects. Supply the track title/creator and permission or licence record for website use. Keep music default off, lazily loaded after the visitor enables sound. A mute control must remain obvious.
